# Pengyu: the path to 1,000 returning people

Owner-operated playbook, prepared November 11, 2026. This document sends nothing, collects nothing and adds no measurement to the app. All rows below are blank templates, not real activity.

## Outcome and counting rules

Keep the target: 1,000 people by December 31, 2026 who make a weekly plan and return in **both December 18–24 and December 25–31**. Long-term mission: an interactive game where people get healthy together, making healthy living, workouts, running and making healthy meals feel less like a chore and more like a privilege, for everyone.

- **Coming:** a person visits Pengyu and makes a weekly plan. A visit alone, download, invitation or promised trial does not count.
- **Returning:** that same person reports using Pengyu in each December window. The windows are the founder's reporting periods; game weeks stay Monday–Sunday.
- This local-only app cannot verify app-wide weekly users. Label external results **voluntary self-reported plan makers and two-window returners**, never verified app-wide WAU. Report respondents and unknowns separately. Do not extrapolate a small willing cohort to every visitor.

### Exact optional ask

> Hi! I'm a CSULB freshman building Pengyu, a little train game for planning workouts. Could I show you a five-minute demo and get your honest feedback? If you like it, could you introduce me to two people who might want to try it?

Five-minute demo: show the first screen, let the person choose their own plan, demonstrate the log/reward with clearly labeled test data, show the postcard preview, then ask where they got stuck. A demonstration log is not a real completed workout or real-user evidence. Do not log a workout for them, promise a health outcome or ask for health details.

Only after a willing connector has tried it, ask:

> Would you like to share the plain invitation with people who might enjoy it? You choose where and how many. No pressure, no rewards for invites, and no need to send me anyone's member list.

Use the existing invitation preview and an explicit Share or Copy invite action. Do not send contacts anything on the founder's behalf. **This coding task authorizes no outreach.**

## Test the 100 connectors × 100 people hypothesis

This is the scale model, not a claim about these clubs:

| Stage | Working assumption | Scenario result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Willing connectors | 100, each able and willing to reach 100 distinct people | 10,000 unique people reached |
| Visit | 50% of reached people visit | 5,000 visitors |
| Make a plan | 40% of visitors make a weekly plan | 2,000 plan makers |
| Return in both windows | 50% of plan makers return during both December windows | 1,000 returning people |

Every rate, each connector's reach and the absence of audience overlap are **untested assumptions**. The final 50% is a single joint two-window return assumption; do not apply it twice or count two separate totals as the same people.

Formula: `returners = deduplicated reach × visit fraction × plan fraction × two-window return fraction`.

At 25% visits, with the other assumptions unchanged, 10,000 reached produces 500 returners; reaching 1,000 then requires 20,000 distinct people reached or higher conversion/return. If only 25% of plan makers return in both windows, the original scenario also produces 500. Recalculate from evidence and reroute distribution or remove friction; keep the 1,000 goal.

Do not call a club's member count reach, call reach visits, add overlapping audiences twice, or call all 100 connectors recruited before they agree. Separate observed demo visits, voluntary self-reports, connector-reported estimates and unknowns.

### Week 1: November 15–21

1. Aim to approach the 15 targets, run five willing demos and recruit 25 voluntary testers. These are activity targets, not forecast results or a replacement for 1,000.
2. Record each actual approach, reply, demo and voluntary introduction. After a willing tester chooses a plan, ask whether they completed it; do not silently watch their screen or record their schedule.
3. After 48–72 hours, compare routes by willing plan makers per founder hour. Replace low-response cold outreach with warm introductions from the kinesiology class, willing club members or friends. If that route is blocked, try moderator-approved student communities, then student creators showing their honest experience, then introductions to nearby-campus groups. No bulk unsolicited messages or invented testimonials.
4. At week's end, replace each measurable assumption with `numerator / denominator`, sample size, dates and method. Keep unavailable visit and reach rates marked **unknown**. A small in-person demo conversion does not establish open-web conversion. Early repeat-use reports are a learning signal, not proof of December retention.
5. Ask willing connectors for introductions to the next connector. Scale the route with the best observed plan-maker yield and lowest founder time cost. Test how many distinct people each can actually reach before assuming 100 each. Recalculate the remaining reach needed; do not fit counts to the target.

### Separate, disclosed check-in

Say before inviting someone into follow-up:

> Pengyu itself has no analytics. Separately, I'm asking volunteers whether they made a plan and used it in two December periods. May I keep your chosen alias and yes/no answers privately, and contact you once for each period through a channel you choose? You can skip any answer or stop at any time. You don't need to share your workouts or health information, and this doesn't affect the game.

Ask once during each window, through their chosen channel:

> Optional Pengyu check-in: have you used Pengyu at least once during December 18–24? Yes, no, or prefer not to answer.

> Optional Pengyu check-in: have you used Pengyu at least once during December 25–31? Yes, no, or prefer not to answer.

Count someone in the final self-reported result only after they report a plan made and **yes in both windows**. A non-response is unknown, never assumed active or assumed a failure. Publish only aggregate totals with their method, denominator, response rate and known limits. In-window answers cover use up to the response time; do not treat an early 'no' as proof they never used it later that week.
